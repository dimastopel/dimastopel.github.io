Imagine a few young men working in the tech industry, let's say somewhere in the Bay area. As most people in their age and industry they struggle to maintain a healthy lifestyle. Eat too much, exercise too little. One day during lunch one of the guys tells others about a new cool startup, called: “Gain with no Pain”. The startup claims to make you fit without exercising. The only thing you need to do is to show up, once a week to their offices and do a quick blood transfusion. As expected everybody is sceptical, but one of them, let’s call him Eric, decides to try it. He comes to their offices, which look excellent, signs up, and starts the weekly procedures. After a couple of months he actually sees good results. He lost weight without losing lean mass. His BMI went back to normal. Amazing. 

A few additional months have passed. Eric is a happy customer. However, there is something unusual that he starts to notice. Almost every time he leaves after the procedure he sees a teenager boy leaving the same office but from the back entrance. He notices it because his bus stop is in the area. The boy uses a bicycle and always tries to leave quickly. One day, the boy falls off the bicycle just after starting the ride, exactly near the bus station where Eric was waiting for the bus. Eric rushed to help him and noticed that the boy was very pale and seemed quite weak. He injured his knee and can ride. Eric helps him to get to his home. The boy, whose name is Victor, lives in a poor neighbourhood with his mom. 

Long story short, Eric and Victor started talking and apparently Victor works at the same startup company. Victor tells Eric that they discovered a way to transfer the benefits of exercising from one person to another via blood transfusion. Victor’s job is to exercise heavily in the morning, rush to the office so that his blood could be transferred to another happy customer. Of course, the startup doesn’t share such not-pretty details with customers.
